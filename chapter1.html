The Need and Scope

The need and scope

In most developing countries, hospital architecture hasn't evolved as a specialized discipline. Hospitals, as all of us are aware, have the specific function of caring for the sick or ensuring well-being. These translate to specific workflows and functional needs in hospitals.

For these to happen well, it warrants a deeper understanding of the space requirements, how they relate to each other and what amenities are required in different functional areas. Broad functions in a typical hospital would be, patient intake or registration, outpatient examination including investigations, laboratories, imaging, interventions including surgery and recovery. In addition, there are support functions like housekeeping, dietary, and administration. Teaching hospitals have academic and research functions as well.

Each of these functions has space requirements with specific design, features and amenities. Some functions like radiology are governed by mandated specifications to eliminate harm from radiation. Similarly, there are safety codes to minimize risks from fire hazards as well as building codes for hospitals. All of this specialized building knowledge has to be brought in while designing an eye hospital as well. Well-designed hospitals add a lot to enhancing patient experience, efficiency and safety to patients and staff.

The guidelines in this manual are meant to provide relevant architectural inputs for those venturing to build an eye care facility and architects working on such projects. As with any such project, it helps to define the boundaries of what this document offers and what it doesn't.

What is not covered

  • Any building project is governed by space availability, either open land or a building that would be converted into an eye care facility. Such constructions or modifications would also be subject to local rules and regulations. The local architect would be or should be well-versed with such information and regulations. These are varied and specific to each project and its location. Thus, these are outside the scope of this manual.
  • A lot of consideration has to go into the decision to build a hospital, then where to build it, size of land or building required and so on. While these do have a bearing on the building and its design, this manual will not be dwelling on this. This manual builds on from the stage that such a decision has been thoughtfully made and the land or appropriate building for remodelling is decided upon.
  • In any hospital, there are areas that don't have hospital specific requirements, such as offices, other administrative areas, meeting rooms, classrooms, auditorium, common toilets, etc. Hence, these are not covered in this document.
  • Since the hospital size would vary, dictated by factors like patient volumes, this manual doesn't attempt to provide the actual layout for complete outpatient services or operating rooms, with the actual number of examination or operating rooms. However, sample layouts are included.

What is covered

  • Several checklists for high-level conceptualization to detailed space planning to ensure that all critical factors and areas considered.
  • Suggested layout of individual rooms for various functions - examination, investigation, surgery, inpatient, etc.
  • Checklists for what all would go into a room as well as other requirements like power, water, data, phone, etc.
